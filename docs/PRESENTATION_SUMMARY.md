## Slide 2: AI Use is Already Widespread

**Key Finding: High Adoption Rate**

| Frequency | Count | Percentage |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Daily | 5 | 22% |
| Several times/week | 11 | 48% |

## Slide 5: Research Confidence is Moderate (Mean: 3.5/5)

**Strongest Confidence Areas**
- Interpreting results against theory: **3.8/5** ★★★★☆
- Documenting sources & decisions: **3.7/5** ★★★★☆
- Maintaining clear argument: **3.7/5** ★★★★☆
- Narrowing a topic: **3.7/5** ★★★★☆

**Weakest Confidence Areas**
- Choosing & justifying method: **3.0/5** ★★★☆☆
- Building systematic search: **3.2/5** ★★★☆☆
- Identifying research gap: **3.4/5** ★★★☆☆

**Interpretation:**
- Students feel confident in **lower-order tasks** (narrowing, documenting)
- **Weaker in higher-order tasks** (methodology, gap identification)
- AI adoption has NOT yet boosted confidence in deep research skills
- This is a key area for pedagogical intervention

| Item | Mean | Status |

## Slide 8: Scientific Practice Knowledge is Mixed

**Good Scientific Practice Scenarios**

**Strong Areas:**
- 91% correctly rejected "AI as author" claim
- 96% understood AI use must be disclosed under applicable rules
- 74% knew to verify AI-suggested references

**Weak Areas:**
- Only 52% knew how to handle contradictory findings
- Responses vary on documentation purpose

**Interpretation:**
- Students have **basic integrity awareness**
- **Gaps in research methodology** (handling contradictions)
- Need for **specific training on research ethics and documentation**

## Slide 9: Open-Ended Challenge #1 — What's Hardest?

**"What is the hardest part of academic writing?"**

**Top Themes:**
1. **Maintaining clear argumentation** (30%) — "keeping the red thread"
2. **Selecting relevant literature** (26%) — deciding what to include
3. **Defining research scope** (22%) — not too broad, not too narrow
4. **Turning information into thesis** (13%) — synthesis challenge

**Student Quotes:**
- "I tend to get lost in details and it's hard to decide what goes in and what's known"
- "Finding the correct research question and the correct path to follow"
- "Separating correct and useful papers from the massive amount available"

**Interpretation:**
- **AI has NOT solved the argumentation problem** — this is still hard
- **Information overload is real** — students need help with curation, not generation
- **Conceptual synthesis** is the bottleneck, not information access

## Slide 10: Open-Ended Challenge #2 — What Would Help?

**"What would make CLARA useful? What would be annoying?"**

**Desired Features:**
1. **Source-based, traceable responses** (52%) — "responses that can be traced to literature with page numbers"
2. **Context retention** (26%) — "understand ongoing conversations"
3. **Concise, actionable answers** (22%) — "short and useful, not repetitive"
4. **Institutional alignment** (17%) — "follow FH Münster rules and guidelines"

**What Annoys Students:**
- Vague or repetitive answers
- Loss of context between interactions
- Responses that don't align with university policy
- Information from outside institutional databases

**Interpretation:**
- Students want **quality over quantity**
- **Trust and transparency** are critical
- **Institutional fit** matters — CLARA must work with, not against, university standards
- **Students expect accountability** from the tool

## Slide 11: Key Takeaways

### ✅ What's Working
- High engagement: 70% use AI weekly or more
- Appropriate use: AI as support, not final output
- Awareness: Students understand basic integrity concerns
- Openness: Students are receptive to AI tools

### ⚠️ What Needs Attention
- **Policy clarity:** Only 29% know university AI rules
- **Methodological confidence:** Weak in research gap & method selection
- **Argumentation:** AI has NOT reduced the hardest part (synthesis)
- **Tool sophistication:** Students want trustworthy, context-aware support

### 🎯 Recommendations
1. **Develop institutional AI use guidelines** — reduce ambiguity
2. **Train on research methodology** — especially gap identification & method selection
3. **Focus on quality over generation** — students need curation, not quantity
4. **Ensure source traceability** — build tools that show their work
5. **Embed in teaching** — make AI support pedagogically aligned
